Non-Cartesian transform#

TL;DR

  • Every NUFFT, in an operator or inside a BART command, is FINUFFT’s on the CPU and cuFINUFFT’s on a CUDA device; BART’s gridder is never built.

  • The transform is computed to a tolerance, \(10^{-3}\) on a grid oversampled by 1.25 by default, set through -o and -w or oversampling and width.

  • The normal operator is a convolution with a point spread function that FINUFFT computes and BART stores and applies.

A radial, spiral or other non-Cartesian readout samples k-space off the grid, so its Fourier transform is a non-uniform FFT (NUFFT) rather than an FFT. You meet it in NUFFT and NoncartesianSense, and inside nufft, pics, nlinv and moba whenever you pass a trajectory. Lesson 7 of the Course computes the transform, density compensation and the point spread function, and lesson 8 reconstructs radial SENSE.

Capabilities#

  • Computes the forward and adjoint NUFFT with FINUFFT on the CPU and cuFINUFFT on a CUDA device, to the transform’s tolerance.

    Code: NUFFT, src/csrc/substitute/finufft.c. Tests: matches an explicit dft on a radial trajectory; adjoint identity holds; a trajectory on a card is transformed by cufinufft (test_finufft.py).

  • Answers every NUFFT BART’s commands build, and builds BART’s gridder zero times.

    Code: src/csrc/substitute/nufft_finufft.c. Tests: nothing builds barts own nufft; barts nufft tool matches an explicit dft with finufft underneath; pics reconstructs the same image either way (test_finufft.py).

  • Refuses a transform FINUFFT cannot serve, with the reason, instead of falling back to BART’s gridder.

    Code: BartError, bartorch_nufft_decline_text. Tests: a transform finufft cannot serve is an error rather than barts gridder; barts own gridder is reachable only from inside the package (test_finufft.py).

  • Takes the oversampling and kernel width you ask for, as BART’s -o and -w or as oversampling and width.

    Code: NUFFT. Tests: barts oversampling is finuffts upsampling; a kernel width asked for buys the accuracy that width buys; precision can be traded for a transform that fits (test_finufft.py).

  • Runs coil calibration (nlinv, ncalib) at a tolerance of \(10^{-6}\) on a grid oversampled by 2.

    Code: _CALIBRATES in _dispatch.py. Tests: a tool that calibrates gets the careful transform (test_finufft.py).

  • Estimates density compensation weights for any trajectory, and carries weights inside the operator.

    Code: estimate_density(), weights= of NUFFT. Tests: the weight rises with radius; it beats the ramp on the image (test_density.py); weights multiply the transform and their conjugate its adjoint (test_finufft.py).

  • Applies the normal operator as a convolution with a point spread function computed by FINUFFT, in every storage BART offers.

    Code: src/csrc/substitute/psf.c, toeplitz=. Tests: the toeplitz normal is the transform pair it stands for; a point spread function is the substituted transforms; every way bart stores a point spread function is served (test_finufft.py).

  • Applies one operator to host and device arrays alike, planning on each side the first time it is used there.

    Code: src/csrc/substitute/nufft_finufft.c. Tests: one operator serves both sides of the bus (test_finufft.py).

Division of responsibility#

  • BART: the NUFFT operator its commands build, and the storage and application of the point spread function’s transfer function.

  • bartorch: the substitution of FINUFFT under BART’s NUFFT and point spread function, the conversion of -o and -w, and density estimation.

  • Other libraries: FINUFFT and cuFINUFFT compute the transform. Gradients with respect to the trajectory or a field map are not computed here (the backward pass of an operator is its adjoint in the image or the samples); mri-nufft provides them.

Spreading, FFT and deapodization#

For an image \(x\) of \(N\) voxels and sample positions \(k_j\) in grid units (multiples of \(1/\mathrm{FOV}\), from \(-n/2\) to \(n/2\)),

\[ y_j = \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}} \sum_{m} x_m \exp\!\Big(-2\pi i \sum_d \frac{k_{j,d}\, m_d}{n_d}\Big), \]

with \(m\) the voxel index about the image centre. This is FINUFFT’s type-2 transform; the adjoint is its type-1 transform with the positive exponent. The forward transform divides the image by the Fourier transform of the kernel (deapodization), zero-pads it to a grid oversampled by \(\sigma\), applies an FFT and interpolates onto the samples with the kernel; the adjoint spreads the samples onto that grid, applies the inverse FFT, crops and deapodizes.[1] FINUFFT chooses the kernel width from the tolerance and \(\sigma\), so -w is carried across as the tolerance that gives that width. cuFINUFFT accepts \(\sigma = 2\) and \(\sigma = 1.25\) only. A \(k_z\) that is zero throughout makes the transform two-dimensional.

Density compensation#

The adjoint is not an inverse: it adds every sample onto the grid, so densely sampled regions are weighted more, and a radial adjoint is blurred by the \(1/\lvert k \rvert\) density. Weights \(d_j \approx 1/\rho(k_j)\) from estimate_density(), the fixed-point iteration of Pipe and Menon,[2] equalize it for a gridding reconstruction. A regularized reconstruction needs none; weights passed to the operator multiply the samples on the forward pass and their conjugate on the adjoint, so the normal is built over \(\lvert w_j \rvert^2\).

Toeplitz normal#

The transform’s normal operator is a convolution with the point spread function

\[ h(m) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_j \lvert w_j \rvert^2 \exp\!\Big(2\pi i \sum_d \frac{k_{j,d}\, m_d}{n_d}\Big), \]

whose support spans twice the image. It is applied exactly on a grid doubled along each axis: zero-pad, FFT, multiply by the transfer function, inverse FFT, crop.[3] FINUFFT computes \(h\) as an adjoint transform of \(\lvert w_j \rvert^2\), and BART stores and multiplies its transfer function in whichever form --nufft-conf selects. toeplitz=False, or pics --no-toeplitz, applies the forward and adjoint transforms instead. The storage forms and the compressed function’s mask are in NUFFT substitution.
