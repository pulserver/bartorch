Encoding operators#

TL;DR

  • Cartesian, non-Cartesian and wave SENSE are one expression, \(y = \sum_a O \cdot T(I \cdot x_a)\), applied by one executor a slab of coils at a time.

  • Subspace, time segmentation, multi-shot phase and SMS are compositions written with @ and +, lowered into that expression when first used.

  • A.plan reports the form an operator was built into; plan.fused is false where a composition fell back to a chain.

The encoding operator \(A\) maps the image you estimate to the samples the scanner acquired, and every reconstruction you assemble yourself starts from one. You build the standard encodings with a constructor, extend them with diagonals, sums and operators of your own, and hand the result to a solver or to autograd as one BART operator. Lesson 6 of the Course builds the SENSE operator, checks its adjoint and extends it with an operator written in Python.

Capabilities#

  • Builds Cartesian, non-Cartesian and wave SENSE as one encoding form, applied a slab of coils at a time.

    Code: CartesianSense(), NoncartesianSense, WaveSense(), src/csrc/ops/sense.c. Tests: every encoding reports its transform; the slab loop is what ran the encoding (test_plan.py); a cartesian reconstruction is the same whatever the slab (test_sense.py).

  • Contracts subspace coefficients with a temporal basis inside the encoding, with a normal kernel that does not grow with the frames.

    Code: basis= of each constructor. Tests: a subspace encoding is the model written out; the collapsed normal does not grow with the frames (test_linop_mri.py); a fused subspace encoding is the frames written out (test_plan.py).

  • Corrects off-resonance by time segmentation around any encoding.

    Code: FieldCorrected(). Tests: more segments is a better approximation; it wraps any encoding; a segmented nufft is the sum of its segments (test_linop_mri.py).

  • Lowers a sum of terms written with @ and + into one encoding, applied in one call however many terms it has.

    Code: linop/_plan.py. Tests: composing builds a description and nothing else; a sum written by hand is the contraction the planner lowers; a composition costs one application however many terms (test_plan.py).

  • Lowers a phase per shot and a phase per slice (SMS) the same way.

    Code: linop/_plan.py, src/csrc/ops/sense.c. Tests: a shot phase is a contraction over the shots; a phase per slice is summed over on the far side; an echo phase with a basis is a contraction over the frames (test_plan.py).

  • Reports the plan an operator was built into, including a fallback to BART’s chain of operators.

    Code: plan. Tests: a plan reads as a sentence; a form the loop cannot take says so; a grid contraction is fused and a coil varying one is not (test_plan.py).

  • Composes, adds, scales, slices, stacks and takes adjoints and normals of operators, and the result stays one BART operator with a correct adjoint.

    Code: LinearOperator, concatenate(), hstack(), block_diag(), block(). Tests: every combination stays one bart operator; the algebra keeps the adjoint honest (test_linop.py); each stays one bart operator (test_linop_combine.py).

  • Takes an operator written in Python, by its forward and adjoint, into a BART composition and a BART solver.

    Code: LinearOperator. Tests: an operator written here chains with one of barts; an operator written here is solved by barts conjugate gradients (test_linop.py).

  • Applies the normal operator through a kernel where one exists, and as the forward followed by the adjoint otherwise.

    Code: gram(), toeplitz=. Tests: a pattern collapses into a kernel; a fused normal is the kernel written out; without toeplitz the normal is the two applications (test_plan.py).

Division of responsibility#

  • BART: the operators the encodings are made of, its chains and sums of operators, the FFT on a device (cuFFT), and the solvers that iterate on the result.

  • bartorch: the encoding executor, the planner that lowers a composition into it, and the callbacks that carry a Python operator into BART.

  • Other libraries: FINUFFT for the NUFFT (Non-Cartesian transform), and mri-nufft for the time-segmentation coefficients FieldCorrected() fits from a field map.

Method#

Encoding form#

For coil \(c\), frame \(t\) and sample \(k\),

\[ y[c, t, k] = \sum_a O[a, t](k)\; T_t\!\left( I[c, a, t](r)\, x[a](r) \right)\!(k). \]

\(I\) holds the coil sensitivities and any spatial weight; \(T\) is the FFT, the NUFFT or the wave transform;[1] \(O\) holds the sampling pattern, density weights, a subspace basis[2] or a sample weight; the sum over \(a\) runs over subspace coefficients, time segments[3] or slices. With \(I = S\), \(T = F\), \(O = P\) and one term this is SENSE, \(A = PFS\).[4] Each application is one call into the library, and the coil images are never held for all coils at once.

Operator algebra#

A @ B applies B first; A + B and A - B need equal shapes; c * A scales by a number, and * between two operators is refused. A.H is the adjoint, A.T the adjoint without conjugation, A.gram() is \(A^H A\) and A.cogram() is \(A A^H\). A[0] restricts the output as tensor indexing does. concatenate() (vertical stacking) applies every operator to one input, hstack() splits the input and adds the outputs, and block_diag() gives each operator its own part of both. An operator of your own defines forward and adjoint, and each application crosses into Python once. Check any adjoint with the dot-product test, \(\langle A x, y \rangle = \langle x, A^H y \rangle\) for random \(x\), \(y\):

lhs = torch.vdot(A(x).flatten(), y.flatten())
rhs = torch.vdot(x.flatten(), A.H(y).flatten())

A product of diagonals around one encoding, or a sum of such products sharing the encoding, is lowered into the form when the operator is first applied, solved, or asked for .plan. Anything else is built as BART’s chain of operators, with the same result at a higher cost; plan.contraction is then "chained" or plan.executor is "chain". The planner’s rules and every field of the plan are in Encoding form and lowering.

Normal operator#

A solver applies \(A^H A\) once per iteration. On a grid with a pattern it is \(\sum_c \overline{S_c} F^H P F S_c\), with a subspace kernel \(K_{aa'} = \sum_t \overline{\Phi_{at}} \Phi_{a't} P_t\) computed once. Along a trajectory the transform’s own normal is a convolution with a point spread function (Non-Cartesian transform). plan.normal names which applies: "kernel", "transform", or "applications", the forward followed by the adjoint.
